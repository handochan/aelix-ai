## 5. Deviations, and why

1. 🔴 **Mutation (e) is not killed by anything in the suite.** §C.1's spelled pins (`chunks ==
   b"EARLY\n"` with `LATE` five graces late, `elapsed < 1.0`, helper ALIVE) cannot see the arming
   instant: this root writes `EARLY` and exits at once, so `last_chunk_at ≈ exited_at` and
   `max(...)` is a no-op for the shape. The mutation table's row (e) names "a `probe_arm.py`-shaped
   assertion inside C.1" — root quiet 0.30 s before exiting, holder writing 0.05 s after — which is
   a **different child shape** and would invert C.1's own byte pin (under it `LATE` is inside the
   grace and IS delivered). The two cannot be one case. I implemented §C.1 as spelled and did not
   add an unbudgeted fifth real-process case. Measured for the reviewer with that shape
   (`probe_arm.py`; root writes `EARLY`, goes quiet 0.30 s, exits; helper writes `LATE` 0.05 s
   later and holds 3 s; 3 runs each): **adopted 0.483-0.489 s, `b'EARLY\nLATE\n'`**; armed from
   `last_chunk_at` alone **0.325-0.329 s, `b'EARLY\n'`**. So the `max(...)` IS load-bearing, and
   nothing in the suite pins it.
2. **Mutation (f) as literally spelled is a near-no-op** (see §3). The killable reading is recorded
   there; nothing in the product changed because of this.
3. `ROOT_WITH_A_LATE_TAIL` gained `.replace("@MARK@", MARK)`. It never had it — its child was
   spawned with the literal string `@MARK@` in its argv — which did not matter while the child died
   during the call. C.1 makes that child outlive the call by ~60 s, so without this the file's own
   "anything these cases leak is greppable" convention would be false for the one child #232 makes
   long-lived. One token; not in the design.
4. §F's "+2 more from C.3 and C.9" is +3 (C.2 is new too): **789**, not 788.
5. Numbers in the ADR/CHANGELOG/README prose are **my** measurements from this lane, not the
   design's, where the two differ by a few ms (4.050 vs 4.036, 13.983 vs 13.939, 4.059 vs 4.044,
   +0.002 vs +0.003 s). Same shapes, same conclusions.
