## 1. What is broken on `main`

The two sites are `tools/bash.py::_LocalBashOperations.exec` (`Popen` + two `to_thread` helpers:
`_drain` reads stdout to EOF, `_wait` does `proc.wait(timeout)`) and
`tools/_subprocess.py::run_cancellable` (`create_subprocess_exec` + `communicate()` under
`wait_for`). Both spawn with `start_new_session=True` and both kill through the pid-only
`kill_process_tree(pid)` — `killpg(SIGKILL)` on POSIX, `taskkill /T /F /PID` on win32.

1. **win32 — the issue's headline, not measurable on this host.** `taskkill /T` follows live
   parent links only. Pi #9129 (auto-closed, unfixed at Pi HEAD — `utils/shell.ts::killProcessTree`
   is still `taskkill`): MSYS bash runs each pipeline stage through a short-lived subshell, so at
   timeout `find`/`xargs`/`head` have a dead parent and survive; `xargs` kept respawning `rg.exe`
   for 70 min. In Aelix that is worse than a leak: `_drain` reads stdout **to EOF**, and the
   survivors hold the pipe, so `exec` does not return until they exit — the tool call hangs past
   its own timeout. ADR-0238 assigns exactly this to #222: "the one place in the tree where the
   shape is a user-visible hang rather than a leaked process".
2. **POSIX pipeline — already contained.** `sleep 30 | cat`, `timeout=1.0`: 1.01 s,
   `timed_out=True`, survivors `[]`. On POSIX the adoption buys one primitive, one `contained`
   rule, one `close()` — and the drain bound below, which is the only POSIX-visible effect.
3. **POSIX `setsid` holder — the drain hangs past the timeout, on all three legs.**
   `python -c 'os.setsid(); print(pid); sleep(8)' & sleep 30`, `timeout=1.0`: `timed_out=True`
   but `exec` returned at **8.02 s**; the critique measured the abort-signal and `CancelledError`
   legs at 8.02 / 8.03 s too. The group kill ended `sh` and `sleep` at 1 s and the drain waited
   7 s for the escapee's copy of the pipe. Without a bound here #222 is inert on the owner's own
   platform.
4. **POSIX exit-0 holder — the success path waits for the helper.** `python -c 'print(pid);
   sleep(4)' & echo started`: root exited at once, `exit_code=0`, `exec` returned at **4.02 s**.
   Pi returns ~0.1 s after exit (`waitForChildProcess`, Pi #5303). **Not this issue** — §H.
5. `run_cancellable` is bounded on every path already (`wait_for` cancels `communicate()`; the
   post-kill `proc.wait()` is under a 2 s guard). Its problem is (1) only, plus a pid-only kill
   issued after asyncio's watcher has reaped the child. Its only callers (`grep._try_ripgrep`,
   `find._try_fd`) spawn one binary with no shell, so no `setsid` holder can exist there.
6. **The bash child inherits Aelix's terminal, and does not stop.** Revision 1 said "`SIGTTIN`,
   the group stops" — wrong for this site: under `start_new_session=True` the child has **no
   controlling terminal**, so the kernel's background-group test never applies (critique,
   measured on a real ctty pty: setsid child `Ss`, `process_group=0` child `T`; #221's and #226's
   measurements were at `process_group=0`). What happens instead is invisible and worse: the
   child (i) competes with the TUI's reader for keystrokes — the child got `'secret\n'`, the TUI's
   `read` got `b''` — and (ii) can `tcsetattr` the user's terminal (ECHO turned off and left off
   after the child exited). `!cat` through `tui/shell.py` → `cli/repl.py:56-61` runs this exact
   `exec` with `signal=None` and no timeout: today it never returns and eats the user's next
   keystroke. Pi spawns with `stdio: ["ignore", "pipe", "pipe"]`. §A.5.

## A. Decision

### A.1 Both sites hold a `ProcessTree`; the pid-only entry point loses its tool callers

- `bash.py`: `subprocess.Popen(argv, cwd=, env=, stdin=DEVNULL (§A.5), stdout=PIPE,
  stderr=STDOUT, **containment_spawn_kwargs(new_session=True))`, then **in the very next
  statement, before any `await`**: `tree = ProcessTree.attach(proc.pid,
  handle=_retained_handle(proc))` (`kill_on_close=False`, the default). `new_session=True`
  keeps today's `setsid`; the ADR's "every tool child made its own session" sentence stays true.
- `_subprocess.py`: `create_subprocess_exec(*args, stdout=PIPE, stderr=PIPE, cwd=,
  **containment_spawn_kwargs(new_session=True))`, then `tree = ProcessTree.attach(proc.pid,
  handle=_retained_handle(proc))` before the first `await` (`_retained_handle` reads the
  asyncio shape through `_transport`). No `stdin=` change here: `rg`/`fd` never read stdin and
  the asymmetry is stated in the module docstring rather than left for a reader to wonder about.
- Every `_kill_group(pid)` / `kill_process_tree(proc.pid)` becomes the tree ladder (§A.2-4/5,
  §A.3). Hard only, as today and as Pi. No `soft_kill` rung.
- `finally: tree.close()` at both sites — a release, never a kill (`kill_on_close=False`: a
  command that exits 0 after backgrounding a daemon keeps it — Pi #8225, ADR-0238's per-site
  rule).
- `bash.py`'s `_kill_group` is **deleted** (a one-line delegate; `tests/tools/test_abort_signal.py`'s
  docstrings that name it are rewritten — prose, not imports).
- `tools/_process_tree.py` (the re-export shim) is **deleted**. Measured (critique): the only
  non-test importers of `kill_process_tree` are the two sites and `rpc/rpc_client.py`, which
  imports the primitive directly; citations *into* the shim are 0. Both sites import
  `ProcessTree, _retained_handle, containment_spawn_kwargs, …` from
  `aelix_ai.utils._process_tree`, as `subprocess_hooks.py` and `rpc_client.py` do.
  `tests/tools/test_process_tree_win32.py` moves its import (line 48) and rewrites its module
  docstring (18-19, 27-32); its `test_bash_kill_group_delegates` /
  `test_subprocess_helper_delegates` (327-336) are **deleted** (the site tests in §C.2/C.3 are
  the replacement). A `Removed` CHANGELOG line names `aelix_coding_agent.tools._process_tree`
  so an out-of-tree importer of the private path has a written signal.
- `kill_process_tree` stays, for `rpc_client.stop()`'s degradation. Its docstring's paragraphs
  about "its two spawn-site callers" and the before-reap / after-reap contrast are rewritten:
  the tool sites hold a tree since #222; the only caller is the rpc degradation.

### A.2 `bash.py::exec`, in order

Every rung of the kill ladder runs on the event-loop thread — measured cost on POSIX 1.6 ms
(critique; two syscalls plus a reap; max heartbeat gap 12 ms = the sleep quantum), on win32 the
pre-existing ≤5 s `taskkill` spawn (`main` runs it on the loop at the same three places today)
plus `proc.wait(reap)` **only when both the job kill and the `TerminateProcess` belt failed**.
The `to_thread` remedy was rejected in #220 review round 2 for opening an unshielded
suspension point; it is not re-adopted here.

1. Resolve env + shell as today. `Popen` as in A.1; `(FileNotFoundError, NotADirectoryError)` →
   `[bash] failed to spawn` + `exit_code=127`, unchanged.
2. `tree = ProcessTree.attach(...)` immediately. `attach` swallows `OSError` into
   `contained=False` on win32 and never raises on POSIX past `_require_pid`; nonetheless the
   attach and the reader start sit inside the same `try` whose `except BaseException` belt is
   `proc.kill()` + re-raise (#221 R2: a spawned child must never be left running by an exception
   between the spawn and the first guarded statement).
3. **The drain moves off `asyncio.to_thread(proc.stdout.read, 4096)`.** Why: after a kill whose
   pipe is held by an escapee (§1-3), a `to_thread` read can be neither cancelled nor abandoned —
   the default executor's threads are non-daemon; measured (critique), an abandoned read held
   `asyncio.run` for the escapee's whole 30 s, and the stdlib path is `THREAD_JOIN_TIMEOUT = 300`
   then an unbounded `_python_exit` join: "the tool call hangs" becomes "Aelix cannot exit".
   `_wait`'s `to_thread(proc.wait, timeout)` stays: it blocks on the **root** only, which the
   belt always ends — the belt is load-bearing for interpreter exit, not only for the reap.

   The replacement is the daemon `_PipeReader` from `aelix_ai/utils/_process_tree.py` (#221),
   extended: `_PipeReader.__init__(stream, state, *, on_chunk=None, on_eof=None)`.
   - With `on_chunk` supplied the reader **does not append to `self.chunks`** — the callback is
     the consumer (invariant, stated in the class docstring; measured cost of retaining twice:
     535 MB vs 285 MB peak RSS on 200 MB of output). The `if self._detached: continue` guard
     stays **above** the callback so nothing is delivered after `detach()`.
   - `on_eof` fires **after the last `on_chunk`, on every exit path** of `run()` (EOF, the
     `except (OSError, ValueError)` leg, and the `finally`).
   - `exec` passes `on_chunk=lambda c: _post(on_data, c)` and `on_eof=lambda: _post(eof.set)`,
     where `_post` is `loop.call_soon_threadsafe` inside `try/except RuntimeError: pass` — a
     closed loop must not raise into the reader thread (`threading.excepthook` printed a
     traceback out of a TUI in the critique's measurement; `rpc_mode.py`'s daemon→loop pump
     already suppresses the same). The `on_data` callback itself runs on the loop thread, as
     today, and checks a loop-side `delivering` flag so **no `on_data` fires after `exec`
     returns** (the flag is cleared in the same statement sequence that returns).
   - **Delivery invariant, stated:** `exec` has called `on_data` for every byte the reader
     accepted before `exec` returns. It holds because chunk callbacks and the EOF callback go
     through the **same** FIFO (`loop._ready`), so `await eof.wait()` cannot resume before every
     earlier chunk callback has run (measured 0/4000 vs 9/3000–15/250 losses for the polled
     `reader.eof` shape revision 1 proposed). On the bounded leg, after `wait_for` times out,
     one `await asyncio.sleep(0)` runs the callbacks already queued before the cut (0/3000 with
     it, 15/250 without).
   - Chunk size becomes `read1(65536)` (checked: every `on_data` in the repo is `chunks.append`
     or a no-op; truncation runs on the joined string; `read1` returns as soon as any bytes are
     available, so it is more prompt than `read(4096)`, not less).
4. `_wait`: `await asyncio.to_thread(proc.wait, timeout)` as today. `TimeoutExpired` →
   `_timed_out = True`, `state.exited_at = time.monotonic()` (the kill instant — whichever leg
   kills first writes it; `_ReadState.exited_at` is documented as exactly that), then **one**
   call `_end_the_tree(tree, proc, reap=REAP_GRACE_SECONDS)` (= `hard_kill` → `proc.kill()`
   belt → `proc.wait(reap)`; **not** a separate `hard_kill` first — that would be two
   `taskkill.exe` spawns on win32). Return `None`. Its `proc.wait(reap)` deliberately races the
   in-flight `to_thread(proc.wait, timeout)` worker on the abort leg; safe under
   `_waitpid_lock` (critique: 20/20 both returned `-9`).
5. Abort watcher: `state.exited_at = now` (if unset), `_end_the_tree(tree, proc,
   reap=INTERRUPT_REAP_SECONDS)`. `CancelledError` leg: the same, then re-raise. **Ordering
   constraint:** the watcher task is cancelled **and awaited** before `tree.close()` —
   `hard_kill` early-returns on `closed`, so a `close()` that beat the watcher would turn a
   firing abort into a silent no-op, a failure today's pid-only `_kill_group` could not have.
6. **The drain, after the root is gone.** Success path (`_wait` returned an exit code):
   `await eof.wait()` — to EOF, unbounded, **deliberately unchanged in this issue** (§H; pinned
   by C.2-8). Kill path (any of the three legs wrote `exited_at`): the awaitable analogue of
   `_drain` on top of the event — idle rule `EXIT_DRAIN_SECONDS` re-armed from
   `max(state.last_chunk_at, state.exited_at)`, under the cap `state.exited_at +
   KILL_DRAIN_SECONDS`, each arm `await asyncio.wait_for(eof.wait(), remaining)`; ends on EOF,
   or idle, or cap, or `not reader.is_alive()` (#221 HC5 — a reader that ended through its
   `except` leg never sets `eof`; `on_eof` in the `finally` covers it too). A flat cap without
   the idle rule would cost Esc a flat 1.0 s where `run_contained` costs ~0.1 s (#221 TP4/PI-2
   recorded the same mistake). No poll: the 5 ms poll revision 1 proposed costs 0.71 % of a core
   and ~163 wakeups/s for the holder's whole life on the success path.
7. **Teardown ordering, each step in its own `finally`, innermost first:** the drain wait is the
   only cancellable step → `reader.detach()` (no await; a single Esc landing during the drain
   skipped it in the critique's measurement, re-opening #221 site-exec-1's retention —
   14.9 GB vs 0 MB after 2 s of a chatty holder) → watcher cancelled+awaited → `tree.close()`
   (outermost, never skipped: a `CancelledError` raised in an inner `finally` propagates into
   the enclosing `try`, measured under 2× and 3× cancels; `weakref.finalize` is the win32
   backstop regardless). `detach()`'s docstring ("called from `run_contained`'s `finally` and
   NOWHERE ELSE") is rewritten for its second caller.
8. Return value semantics unchanged: `ExecExitResult(exit_code=None, timed_out=…)` on either
   kill; abort wins the label.

### A.3 `_subprocess.py::run_cancellable`, in order

`create_subprocess_exec` with the containment kwargs → `tree.attach` before the first `await` →
the existing `wait_for(communicate())` → on `TimeoutError`: `tree.hard_kill()` + the belt
`proc.kill()` under `suppress(ProcessLookupError)` (asyncio's `Process.kill` raises it on an
exited child) + the existing bounded `wait_for(proc.wait(), 2)`; on `CancelledError`: the same
and re-raise; `finally: tree.close()`. (`Process.wait()` is a pipe-EOF wait, not a process wait —
irrelevant here because no holder outside the tree can exist at this site, §1-5, and the
`returncode is None` guard the critique proposed was measured a no-op.) The ladder addresses the
**pgid captured at attach** and never calls `os.getpgid` after asyncio's watcher reaped the
child — that is what changes at this site.

### A.4 What each kill is, per platform

| | POSIX | win32 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tree | `killpg(pgid, SIGKILL)` on the group the spawn asked for | `taskkill /T /F` FIRST (root alive → walks escapees), then `TerminateJobObject` (parentless members — the #9129 leaves) |
| belt | `proc.kill()`; the invariant is *nothing else reaps this pid and the belt is issued before any reap* (critique: 150/150 with a co-waiter). bpo-38630's poll is **not** the protection here — it never reaps while `_wait` holds `_waitpid_lock` (0/50) | `TerminateProcess` on the retained handle; the only thing that ends the root where the attach failed and `taskkill.exe` did not resolve |
| holder outside the tree | a `setsid` descendant survives (the reaper's walk, ADR-0238); the drain is bounded so `exec` returns | none — a job holds every descendant. The bound is a belt for the failed-attach case; whether it is ever armed there is measurement 7 in §J |
| `close()` | nothing | `CloseHandle(job)`; `kill_on_close=False` so nothing dies |
| loop-block | 1.6 ms | ≤5 s `taskkill` spawn (pre-existing) + `reap` only when both kills failed |

### A.5 `stdin=DEVNULL` for the bash child (Pi `stdio: ["ignore", …]`)

Adopted, for §1-6's reasons (keystroke theft and `tcsetattr` from a tool child; `!cat` never
returning), not revision 1's. Stated per platform, in #221 R4's words: on POSIX `DEVNULL` +
`setsid` makes the child non-interactive — `cat` prints nothing and exits 0, `git commit`
without `-m` fails with "no message". On win32 `containment_spawn_kwargs` returns only
`CREATE_NEW_PROCESS_GROUP`, so the child keeps Aelix's console: a real stdin reader gets EOF
from `NUL`, but a program that reads `CONIN$` directly (`git` credential prompts) or `Read-Host`
still prompts there and still costs the whole timeout — and only when `default_timeout != 0`
(`bash.py` documents `0` as Pi's unbounded mode). Print/rpc/json modes: stdin is a protocol
stream and a shell child reading it would eat protocol bytes — `DEVNULL` is right there too.
There is no mode in which the bash child legitimately reads Aelix's stdin. Its own CHANGELOG
sentence, its own site test (C.2-6), and — because `!command` in the TUI goes through this
`exec` — **rule 9 applies**: a `uv run aelix` session before and after (§G.2).

### A.6 What is accepted, stated

- The job-assignment window: both spawn shapes attach ~0.1–0.3 ms apart on POSIX (critique,
  120 spawns each) and both **before** a python child's first instruction; a `/bin/sh` child's
  first grandchild is +3.1 ms past the `Popen` attach point. The proactor window on win32 is
  unmeasured (module docstring) and structurally wider because the transport's pipe-connection
  round trips sit inside the spawn `await` — the one honest reason `Popen` is kept at the bash
  site (§I.1). The `taskkill`-first belt covers an escapee whose parent is still alive either
  way.
- On win32 the child no longer sees a console Ctrl+C (`CREATE_NEW_PROCESS_GROUP`), as at every
  other containment site since #220; the cancel/abort leg ends it, and that leg is now a job kill
  rather than a `taskkill`. Accepted in #220 W14 and #221 WIN-7/8; measured in the critique that
  the cancel leg still ends the whole tree.
- A `setsid` descendant on POSIX still survives the bash tool's kill (ADR-0238: the reaper's
  walk). What changes is that it no longer hangs the tool: Esc with a surviving holder costs
  ~0.1 s (idle rule), at most `KILL_DRAIN_SECONDS`.
- The post-kill bound cuts output a chatty escapee is still writing after the idle window. The
  command was killed; its bytes are a courtesy, as for `run_contained`.
- A detached reader keeps one daemon thread and one fd alive until the holder closes the pipe
  (#221 R1's stated leak, now at a fourth site). `detach()` keeps reading so the holder is never
  wedged on a full pipe.
- #230's policy question (an abort landing during the post-exit drain) exists at this site too;
  #222 preserves today's behaviour and pins nothing about it, so #230 stays free to choose.
- `_LocalBashOperations` is the swap surface; a custom `operations` (SSH) is untouched.

## C. Tests

Conventions for every real-process case: children write bytes through `sys.stdout.buffer`
(win32 CRLF, handoff-221 §3-12); child scripts are files under `tmp_path`, invoked as
`f'& "{sys.executable}" "{script}" …'` on win32 / `shlex.quote` on POSIX (`test_abort_signal.py`'s
technique — the resolved shell is pwsh on the windows leg); pids are registered through the
`_Registrar` + `os.replace` marker protocol of `test_run_contained_real_processes.py` so a call
that never returns leaves no 60 s stray; `MARK = "aelix222"` in every argv; `OUTLIVE = 60.0`;
bounds are `bound = timeout + KILL_DRAIN_SECONDS + 1.5`, `+= 5.0` on win32, **never** `+
REAP_GRACE_SECONDS` in the asserted `elapsed` (only in the outer `_run_bounded` watchdog);
`warnings.warn(f"... {elapsed:.3f}s")` in every timing case so the windows log carries numbers;
no `skipif` — both arms asserted, the asymmetry documented.

### C.1 The zombie-leader flip (answer to I.4)

`test_kill_process_tree_reaches_the_group_of_a_zombie_leader` keeps its behaviour on both
platforms; **only its docstring flips**: "the shape `bash.py`'s callers are in" → "the shape
`rpc_client.stop()`'s degradation is in", and the win32 sentence → "which is why that degradation
is a degradation; the tool sites hold a tree since #222". No twin:
`test_a_descendant_whose_parent_already_exited_still_dies` already attaches a tree, lets the
parent exit, `hard_kill()`s and asserts the grandchild dead unconditionally on both platforms
(ADR-0238 names it as the #9129 pin); the twin's deltas are inert. The site-level proof is C.2-1.

### C.2 New: `tests/tools/test_bash_tool_containment.py` (real children, both platforms)

1. **timeout ends a tree whose intermediate parent has exited** (the #9129 shape): root python
   spawns intermediate python which spawns the sleeper (`OUTLIVE`, inherits the pipe —
   `stdout=sys.stdout, stderr=sys.stderr` explicitly, so win32 duplicates the handle) and exits;
   root sleeps `OUTLIVE`; `timeout=1.0` (the runner's pwsh chain reaches the third pid in
   ~0.3 s; `test_run_contained_real_processes.py` uses 1.0 for the same shape and is green on both
   windows legs). Assert `exec` returned within `bound`, `timed_out=True`, sleeper DEAD on both
   platforms. Discriminates on both legs — the C.5(a) mutant leaves the sleeper alive on POSIX
   too (critique, measured); `main` is green on POSIX only because `killpg` reaches it.
2. **abort (signal) does the same** — same tree; wait for the sleeper's pid file (not a blind
   0.3 s — pwsh starts in 0.5–0.57 s on the runner), assert it ALIVE, then `sig.abort()`.
3. **`CancelledError` does the same** — same tree, same pid-file wait, `task.cancel()`.
4. **`setsid` holder: bounded on both arms.** Root spawns a `setsid` python holding stdout for
   `OUTLIVE`; `timeout=0.5`. Assert `elapsed <= bound` on both arms; POSIX: `timed_out=True`,
   holder ALIVE (documented; `strays` reaps it); win32: holder DEAD (the job reaches it).
   4b/4c: the same on the abort-signal and `CancelledError` legs (all three legs hang on `main`;
   the same-group tree of 1–3 returns at 0.6 s with no bound at all and cannot kill "arm the
   bound on the timeout leg only").
5. **exit 0 with a backgrounded helper keeps the helper** (`kill_on_close=False`): the helper is
   spawned with **all three** `stdin/stdout/stderr=DEVNULL` (`stderr=STDOUT` at the spawn means
   fd 2 is the same pipe — a helper with only stdout redirected held `exec` for 20 s in the
   critique's measurement); `_run_bounded`; assert `exit_code=0`, returned promptly, helper alive
   on both platforms.
6. **stdin is `/dev/null`** (A.5): `_with_planted_stdin` (fd 0 carries bytes; `os.fstat(0)`
   self-check) → child prints `repr(sys.stdin.read())` → `''`. Discriminates on POSIX; the win32
   arm is a shape check (win32 stdin comes from `GetStdHandle`, not fd 0) whose real guard is C.4.
   No `tcsetattr`/ENOTTY assertion (vacuous on a planted pipe).
7. **the tree is released, not killed, on success**: a spy through a monkeypatched
   `ProcessTree.attach` (wrapping the real one) records `contained is True`, `hard_kill` not
   called, `closed is True` after return, and `attach` preceded the reader start and the first
   `proc.wait` (the ordering assertion that replaces revision 1's unkillable "attach after the
   first await" mutation).
8. **a successful root with a short-lived pipe holder still drains to EOF**: root writes `EARLY`
   and exits 0; the helper (inherited stdout) writes `LATE` after 0.5 s and exits. Assert
   `exit_code == 0`, `b"".join(chunks) == b"EARLY\nLATE\n"` (bytes), under a `wait_for` hang
   detector. Pins §H's out-of-scope decision.
9. **delivery completeness**: a chatty root (≥2 MB in many writes) under a loaded loop (a
   busy task ticking); assert `b"".join(chunks)` byte-equal to `subprocess.run` of the same
   argv. Kills "deliver EOF through a separate path from the chunks" and "drop the post-cut
