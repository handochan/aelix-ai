## §I — recommendations for the five questions

**I.1 — Keep `subprocess.Popen` + the daemon `_PipeReader` (A.2-3 as designed, with the delivery fix).**
The premise for moving off `asyncio.to_thread` is measured and stronger than stated: an abandoned `to_thread(proc.stdout.read, …)` held `asyncio.run` for the escapee's full 30 s, and the stdlib path is `THREAD_JOIN_TIMEOUT = 300` then an unbounded `_python_exit` join — "the tool call hangs" becomes "Aelix cannot exit". But the two reasons §I.1 offers for preferring `Popen` over `create_subprocess_exec` are both wrong and must be replaced: the attach window is measured at 0.99 ms vs 1.29 ms on POSIX with **both** shapes attaching before a python child's first instruction (ASYNC-6), and the `on_data` cadence is not a differentiator (1857 vs 2092 deliveries on 4 MB, same 0.021 s completion). The reason that survives is win32-only and honest: the proactor transport's pipe-connection round trips sit **inside** the spawn `await`, so the assignment window there is unmeasured and structurally wider, which is the window this issue exists to close — and `Popen` lets the job be assigned in the statement immediately after the spawn returns. ASYNC-3's attempt to add "asyncio flow control wedges the holder" as a third reason is refuted (the asyncio analogue of `detach()` kept the holder advancing ~45,000 writes/2 s), so do not use it.

**I.2 — Adopt `stdin=DEVNULL`; there is no mode in which the bash child legitimately reads Aelix's stdin.**
Print/rpc/json modes: stdin is a protocol stream and a shell child reading it would eat protocol bytes. TUI mode: measured, the child does not stop on `SIGTTIN` (no controlling terminal under `setsid`) — it *competes with the TUI for keystrokes* (`CHILD-GOT:typed-by-the-user` while the TUI's own `read` got nothing) and can `tcsetattr` the user's terminal (ECHO left off after the child exited). `!cat` through `cli/repl.py:62` has no timeout and no abort signal, so today it never returns. That is strictly worse than the stop the design describes, so adopt — but with POSIX-4's corrected mechanism, WIN32-2's win32 clause (`CONIN$`/`Read-Host` still prompt; the timeout, when non-zero, is what bounds them there), and SCOPE-2's rule-9 live check.

**I.3 — Reuse `KILL_DRAIN_SECONDS` as the cap *and* `EXIT_DRAIN_SECONDS` as the idle rule; take no new constant.**
A flat cap makes Esc with a surviving holder cost a flat 1.0 s where `run_contained` costs ~0.1 s, and #221 already recorded the identical mistake ("a flat 5 s … and bought zero bytes"; ~1.1 s with the idle rule). The design's version also drops `_drain`'s `not reader.is_alive()` half, which #221 HC5 added after measuring 0.102 s vs 0.000 s for a reader that ended through its `except` leg. Express it as the awaitable analogue of `_drain` on top of the EOF event — cap `state.exited_at + KILL_DRAIN_SECONDS`, idle window re-armed from `max(last_chunk_at, exited_at)`, `await asyncio.wait_for(eof_event.wait(), remaining)` per arm — not as a second exit condition written from scratch at a fourth site, and not as a 5 ms poll (which costs 0.71% of a core and ~163 wakeups/s for the holder's whole life on the success path §H leaves unbounded).

**I.4 — Keep the pid-only test, drop the twin; the flip is the docstring alone.**
`test_a_descendant_whose_parent_already_exited_still_dies` (`test_process_tree_real_processes.py:218-236`) already is the #9129 shape with `ProcessTree.attach` + `hard_kill` and asserts the grandchild dead unconditionally on both platforms; ADR-0238:294 names it as that pin. The twin's deltas (unreaped leader, `new_session=True`) are provably inert — `hard_kill` reads only the job/pgid captured at attach, and both spawn modes dispatch identically. Five reviewers converge here. Say in §C.1 that the site-level assertion is §C.2-1, so the docstring flip is not mistaken for the proof.

**I.5 — Deleting the shim is safe; add a written signal anyway.**
Measured: the only non-test importers of `kill_process_tree` are the two sites being changed plus `rpc/rpc_client.py`, which already imports from `aelix_ai.utils._process_tree`; citations *into* the shim are 0. The module is private and undocumented in the guides. Delete it, and add a `Removed` clause to the CHANGELOG naming `aelix_coding_agent.tools._process_tree`, plus the two prose sites no gate catches (`aelix_agents/reaper.py:105-108`, `SLICE-STATUS.md:39`'s `Where` column and `:116`).
